The city noise fades about twenty minutes past the last roundabout. Then the road climbs onto a plateau of bare, honey-coloured hills, and the High Atlas comes into view, white along the top for much of the year.

That is the Agafay Desert, Marrakech's own desert: about 30–40 km south-west of the medina, or 45 minutes to an hour by road. You can ride a camel at sunset, take a quad over the ridges, have dinner under the stars, or stay the night in a desert camp, all without the 8–10 hour drive to the Sahara dunes. You won't find sand dunes there, though. Agafay is a stone desert. This guide covers the camps, the quads and the dinner tours, what each one costs, and which one suits your trip.

Key takeaways

01

Agafay is 45–60 minutes from Marrakech, so you can visit in an afternoon. Merzouga's dunes take 2–3 days.

02

It is rocky, not sandy. If golden dunes are the point of your trip, Agafay will disappoint you.

03

What the Agafay Desert is (and isn't)

Agafay is a high, dry plateau of rounded hills, gravel and scrub, with the Atlas Mountains as a backdrop. Geographers call it a reg, a rocky desert, as opposed to an erg, a sea of sand dunes like the Sahara at Merzouga. In the low evening light it looks more like the moon than the Sahara. You will also hear it called the Agafay valley, and several camps use that name. It is the same plateau.

People who arrive expecting the photos from Erg Chebbi are disappointed. People who come for the space, the silence and the sunset usually call it the best evening of their trip.

Agafay or Merzouga?

The question is really how much time you have. Merzouga is a 2–3 day round trip from Marrakech, and most of that time is spent in a van crossing the Atlas. Agafay takes an afternoon. If you have three or four nights in Marrakech, as many first-time visitors do (see how many days to spend in Marrakech), Agafay is the practical choice. If dunes are the reason you came to Morocco, plan the longer trip using our Sahara desert tours from Marrakech guide.

Rule of thumb: fewer than six days in Morocco, choose Agafay. A week or more with a few days to spare, choose Merzouga. You can do both: Agafay early in the trip, the Sahara later.

Agafay desert camps: staying the night

This is the part day-trippers miss. When the evening tours drive back to Marrakech around ten, Agafay goes silent. Without the city's glow, the sky fills with stars, and you wake to sunrise on the Atlas from your tent. A single night here works well between two stays in a riad, or as the quiet end of a busy week in the medina.

From Berber tents to luxury camps

Agafay's camps fall into roughly three tiers:

  • Simple Berber-style camps. Canvas tents, shared or basic private bathrooms, dinner and breakfast included. Most atmosphere for the price.
  • Mid-range camps. Proper beds, private bathrooms in every tent, often a small pool. The best value for most couples.
  • Agafay luxury camps. Large lodge-style tents or domes, heated in winter, pools with Atlas views, set-menu dinners and spa treatments. Popular for honeymoons. See our Morocco honeymoon guide.

Most camps can arrange a transfer from Marrakech, and many include dinner, a camel ride or a quad session in the rate. Compare what's included, not just the room price. For a camp you can browse and book with free cancellation, search Agafay camps on Booking.com and filter by "Agafay" and review score.

Local tip

In December and January, ask the camp whether tents are heated before you book. Desert nights get properly cold, and an unheated canvas tent is a long night, however good the stars are.

Agafay desert quad and buggy tours

If your group wants some adrenaline, this is the one to book. Sessions run one or two hours on the desert tracks, usually with a tea stop in a Berber village. You can book them alone or combine them with a camel ride and dinner for a full afternoon. Buggies seat two people and usually cost more per vehicle than quads. They suit couples where only one person wants to drive. Our quad biking page covers what to wear and what to expect.

The sunset camel ride and dinner

The classic Agafay evening, and the cheapest way to see the desert. Times change with the seasons because everything is planned around sunset. Pickups are mid-afternoon in winter and early evening in summer. The order of events stays the same:

1
Pickup in Marrakech
Roughly 2–3 hours before sunset

A minibus or 4x4 collects you from your hotel or the nearest drop-off point to your riad, then picks up other guests. Allow 45–60 minutes of driving once everyone is on board.

2
Camel ride into the sunset
About an hour, at walking pace

Camels are led in a line, so you don't need any experience. Lean back as the camel stands up and kneels down. That is the only tricky moment. The ride ends on a ridge just as the light turns gold.

3
Dinner at the camp
Usually two to three hours

Mint tea when you arrive, then Moroccan salads, a tagine or couscous, and fruit. Music and a fire show follow, and the lanterns go down so you can see the stars. Mention vegetarian or other dietary needs when you book, not at the table.

4
Back in Marrakech
Typically around 10–11 pm

Same drop-off point as the pickup. If you have an early flight or a balloon ride the next morning, check the return time before you book.

"Dinner show" can mean a quiet camp with twenty guests or a show tent with two hundred. If you want quiet, look for "small group" in the title and a cap on group size in the description.

Local tip

If you are staying inside the medina, your pickup will not be at your door. Vans can't fit through the alleys. Ask your riad the evening before where the nearest drop-off point is, and allow ten minutes to walk there with someone from the riad.

A slower option: the full-day 4x4 tour

For families, older travellers, or anyone who would rather look at the scenery than ride over it. A private 4x4 crosses the plateau, stops in a Berber village for tea and bread, includes a short camel ride and lunch in a tent, and often visits Lalla Takerkoust lake on the way back. Getting to Agafay from Marrakech

Tours and most camps include the drive, so most people never need to arrange it. If yours doesn't, you have three options:

  • A pre-booked private transfer. The simplest option for camp stays, because many camps sit at the end of unsigned tracks that a city taxi won't know. Kiwitaxi lets you fix the price before you travel.
  • Your camp's own transfer. Often the easiest. Ask for the price when you book.
  • Driving yourself. The road to Lalla Takerkoust is paved, but the last stretch to most camps is gravel. Read our guide to renting a car in Morocco first.

Before you go: Summer afternoons in Agafay often reach the high 30s °C, and winter nights can drop to single figures. Pack a warm layer, a scarf for dust, and closed shoes. Our Marrakech packing list covers the rest.

Planning the rest of your stay? Agafay pairs well with one of the best day trips from Marrakech. Sunset in the desert one day, Essaouira or the Ourika Valley the next.

Questions

01How far is the Agafay Desert from Marrakech?

About 30–40 km south-west of the medina, which is 45 minutes to an hour by road, depending on traffic and your camp. That is why most people visit in an afternoon or stay a single night.

02Is Agafay a real desert?

Yes. It is a dry, stony desert, not a sandy one. You will see rolling bare hills and gravel plains, not dunes. For sand dunes you need to go to Merzouga or Zagora, which are much further from Marrakech.

03Is it worth staying overnight in an Agafay camp?

If you can spare one night, yes. The evening tours leave around ten, and after that you get the silence, the stars and the sunrise over the Atlas. Those are the parts most people remember. A mid-range camp with dinner and breakfast included is usually the best value.

04Is the Agafay Desert good for kids?

Very. The short drive, the gentle camel rides and the early dinner work well for families. Ask about quad age limits before you book. A full-day private tour gives you the most flexibility. See our Marrakech with kids guide for more.

05When is the best time of year to visit Agafay?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) have warm days and mild evenings. Winter is fine with a warm jacket and a heated tent. In high summer, choose sunset activities and avoid quads in the middle of the day. Our best time to visit Morocco guide has month-by-month detail.
