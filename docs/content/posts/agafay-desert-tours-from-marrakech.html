The city noise fades about twenty minutes past the last roundabout. Then the road climbs onto a plateau of bare, honey-coloured hills, and the High Atlas comes into view, white along the top for much of the year.

That is the Agafay Desert, the closest desert to Marrakech: about 40 km south-west of the medina, or 45 minutes to an hour by road. An Agafay desert tour from Marrakech gives you camels, a desert sunset and dinner under the stars without the 8–10 hour drive to the Sahara dunes. You won't find sand dunes there, though. Agafay is a stone desert. This guide covers the four kinds of tour, what each one costs, and which one suits your trip.

Key takeaways

01

Agafay is 45–60 minutes from Marrakech, so you can visit in an afternoon. Merzouga's dunes take 2–3 days.

02

It is rocky, not sandy. If golden dunes are the point of your trip, Agafay will disappoint you.

03

The sunset camel ride with dinner is the cheapest and most popular tour. Quads and full-day 4x4 tours cost more.

04

Bring a warm layer. After sunset the temperature drops quickly in every season.

Is the Agafay Desert worth it?

Yes, if you know what you are getting. Agafay is a high, dry plateau of rounded hills, gravel and scrub, with the Atlas Mountains as a backdrop. In the low evening light it looks more like the moon than the Sahara. People who arrive expecting the photos from Erg Chebbi are disappointed. People who come for the space, the silence and the sunset usually call it the best evening of their trip.

Agafay or Merzouga?

The question is really how much time you have. Merzouga, with its tall sand dunes on the edge of the Sahara, is a 2–3 day round trip from Marrakech. Most of that time is spent in a van crossing the Atlas. Agafay takes an afternoon. If you have three or four nights in Marrakech, as many first-time visitors do (see how many days to spend in Marrakech), Agafay is the practical choice. If dunes are the reason you came to Morocco, plan the longer trip using our Sahara desert tours from Marrakech guide.

Rule of thumb: fewer than six days in Morocco, choose Agafay. A week or more with a few days to spare, choose Merzouga. You can do both: Agafay early in the trip, the Sahara later.

The four kinds of Agafay tour

There are dozens of listings, and nearly all of them are a version of one of four formats. Decide on the format first and choosing a listing gets much easier.

1. Sunset camel ride and dinner show

This is the classic and the most booked. You are picked up in the afternoon, ride a camel for about an hour as the sun goes down, then have dinner in a lantern-lit camp: salads, a tagine, fruit and mint tea, followed by drumming, often a fire show, and stargazing. It is the cheapest Agafay tour and the easiest to fit into any day. Read the listing carefully: "dinner show" can mean a quiet camp or a crowded venue with several hundred guests.

2. Quad bike or buggy

One- or two-hour sessions on the desert tracks, usually with a stop for tea in a village. You can book them alone, or combine them with a camel ride and dinner as a full afternoon. Buggies seat two people and usually cost more per vehicle than quads. They suit couples where only one person wants to drive. Our quad biking page covers what to wear and what to expect.

3. Full-day private 4x4 tour

A slower day: hotel pickup in the morning, a drive across the plateau, tea and bread in a Berber village, a camel ride, lunch in a tent, and often a stop at Lalla Takerkoust lake on the way back, with the mountains behind it. Costs more per person because it is usually private. It is the best choice for families, older travellers, and anyone who would rather look at the scenery than ride over it. If you prefer mountains, our Atlas Mountains day trips guide has similar full-day options.

4. A night in a desert camp

Agafay's camps range from simple Berber tents to lodges with private bathrooms, pools and set-menu dinners. Staying overnight gets you something the evening tours can't: the stars after the day visitors leave, and a silent sunrise. Many camps arrange transfers from Marrakech, so this also works as a single night between two stays in a riad. Compare it with the other options in our where to stay in Marrakech guide.

Local tip

If you are staying inside the medina, your pickup will not be at your door. Vans can't fit through the alleys. Ask your riad the evening before where the nearest drop-off point is, and allow ten minutes to walk there with someone from the riad.

How a sunset-and-dinner tour runs

Times change with the seasons, because everything is planned around sunset. Pickups are mid-afternoon in winter and early evening in summer. The order of events stays the same:

1
Pickup in Marrakech
Roughly 2–3 hours before sunset

A minibus or 4x4 collects you from your hotel or the nearest drop-off point to your riad, then picks up other guests. Allow 45–60 minutes of driving once everyone is on board.

2
Camel ride into the sunset
About an hour, at walking pace

Camels are led in a line, so you don't need any experience. Lean back as the camel stands up and kneels down. That is the only tricky moment. The ride ends on a ridge just as the light turns gold.

3
Dinner at the camp
Usually two to three hours

Mint tea when you arrive, then Moroccan salads, a tagine or couscous, and fruit. Music and a fire show follow, and the lanterns go down so you can see the stars. Mention vegetarian or other dietary needs when you book, not at the table.

4
Back in Marrakech
Typically around 10–11 pm

Same drop-off point as the pickup. If you have an early flight or a balloon ride the next morning, check the return time before you book.

How to pick a good operator

Every Agafay listing promises the same sunset, so compare them on these points:

  • Pickup. Check that it is included and covers your area. Some cheap listings charge for transport separately.
  • Group size. A small camp with twenty guests is a very different evening from a show tent with two hundred.
  • Return time. Matters if you have an early start the next day.
  • Animal welfare. Recent reviews will tell you whether the camels look healthy, rested and well handled. If they don't, choose another tour.
  • Quad rules. Minimum ages and passenger rules for children vary by operator. Check before booking a family trip.
  • Cancellation. Choose free cancellation. Dust storms and occasional winter rain do cancel tours.

Before you book: Summer afternoons in Agafay often reach the high 30s °C, and winter nights can drop to single figures. Pack a warm layer, a scarf for dust, and closed shoes. Our Marrakech packing list covers the rest.

Questions

01How far is the Agafay Desert from Marrakech?

About 40 km south-west of the medina, which is 45 minutes to an hour by road, depending on traffic and your camp. That is why most people visit in an afternoon instead of staying overnight.

02Is Agafay a real desert?

Yes. It is a dry, stony desert, not a sandy one. You will see rolling bare hills and gravel plains, not dunes. For sand dunes you need to go to Merzouga or Zagora, which are much further from Marrakech.

03Can I visit Agafay without a tour?

You can hire a private driver for the day and book a camp directly, and some camps accept day visitors for lunch or the pool. The camps are reached by unsigned tracks, though, so for one evening a tour with pickup is usually simpler and often cheaper.

04Is the Agafay Desert good for kids?

Very. The short drive, the gentle camel rides and the early dinner work well for families. Ask about quad age limits before you book. A full-day private tour gives you the most flexibility. See our Marrakech with kids guide for more.

05When is the best time of year to visit Agafay?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) have warm days and mild evenings. Winter is fine with a warm jacket. In high summer, choose a sunset tour and avoid quad sessions in the middle of the day. Our best time to visit Morocco guide has month-by-month detail.
